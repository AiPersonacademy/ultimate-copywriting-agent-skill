## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

### The Life-Force 8 (Whitman)
Eight biologically hardwired desires that drive all purchasing — stronger than any learned want:
1. **Survival** & life extension
2. **Enjoyment** of food & beverages
3. **Freedom** from fear, pain, danger
4. **Sexual** companionship
5. **Comfortable** living conditions
6. **Superiority** — winning, status, keeping up
7. **Protection** of loved ones
8. **Social approval** & belonging

**Use**: Lead every piece of copy with an appeal to at least one LF8 drive. The strongest ads tap into hardwired biology, not learned preferences.

### The Slippery Slide (Sugarman)
Every element of an ad exists for one purpose: to get the reader to read the *next* element. Headline → first sentence → second sentence → CTA. If any element breaks the slide, the reader exits.

**Use**: After writing, read top-to-bottom asking: "Does this sentence make the next sentence irresistible?" Cut everything that doesn't pull forward.

### The Tension-Desire-Action Loop (Whitman)
Unmet Need → Psychological Tension → Desire → Action to Satisfy Desire. Advertising doesn't create desire — it channels pre-existing desire toward a specific product.

**Use**: Identify which need is already unmet, amplify the tension with vivid specifics, then present your product as the resolution.

### Mental Movies (Whitman)
Use specific, sensory, visual language to make prospects *experience* the product inside their heads before they buy. The first use of any product is inside the consumer's mind.

**Use**: Replace abstract claims with concrete imagery. "Go somewhere and do something" → "Walk into your sunlit kitchen, pull out the crispiest, most golden pizza you've ever tasted."

### The Cleverectomy Principle (Whitman)
Never sacrifice clarity for cleverness. Puns, wordplay, and "creative" headlines destroy response rates. Plain, punchy, benefit-loaded directness wins every time.

**Use**: After writing a headline, ask: "If I removed the clever twist, would it still work?" If not, the cleverness IS the headline — and it shouldn't be.

### Elaboration Likelihood Model — ELM (Whitman)
Two persuasion routes: **Central** (logic, evidence, comparison — for high-involvement buyers) and **Peripheral** (authority cues, social proof, visual appeal — for low-involvement buyers). Match your approach to the reader's engagement level.

### Reason-Why Copy (Hopkins)
Give concrete, specific reasons for every claim. "The best soap" fails. "We tested 347 formulas over 18 months to find the one with the richest lather and gentlest pH" sells.

### Cure vs. Prevention (Sugarman/Hopkins)
Products positioned as solutions to existing problems outsell preventative products. Reframe prevention as cure: not "protect against burglary" but "your neighbor was robbed last week."

### Seven Steps to Great Copy (Sugarman)
1. Become an expert on the product
2. Know your prospect's deepest motivations
3. Write 25+ headline candidates
4. Write the first draft freely — no editing
5. Edit ruthlessly for clarity and flow
6. Incubate — walk away for 24+ hours
7. Final review with fresh eyes
