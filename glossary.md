# Glossary — Ultimate Copywriting Skill

**Amortization** — Reframing a price as a tiny daily cost ("Just $1.25 a day") to reduce sticker shock (Ch 4, 18)

**Assumed Constraints** — Self-imposed creative limitations that prevent you from seeing better solutions; break them deliberately (Ch 13, 15)

**Authority Positioning** — Establishing yourself as the expert in your field so prospects defer to your recommendations (Ch 6, 17)

**Bandwagon Effect** — Leveraging the perception that "everyone is doing it" to reduce purchase risk and increase action (Ch 1, 6)

**Belief Re-ranking** — Shifting which attributes a consumer considers most important when evaluating your product (Ch 1, 5)

**Buying Environment** — The overall atmosphere created by the ad's layout, tone, and design that predisposes the reader to purchase (Ch 3, 8, 12)

**Cleverectomy** — Whitman's term for removing all cleverness, puns, and wordplay from copy in favor of direct, benefit-loaded clarity (Ch 2, 9)

**Copy Lures** — Whitman's 12 techniques for pulling readers deeper into body copy after the headline (Ch 3)

**Copy Sequence** — Sugarman's prescribed order for ad elements: headline → subhead → photo/caption → body → close → CTA (Ch 8)

**Credibility Transfer** — Borrowing trust from an authority, institution, or celebrity and attaching it to your product (Ch 6)

**Cure vs. Prevention** — Hopkins' and Sugarman's principle that products positioned as solutions to existing problems outsell preventative products (Ch 16)

**Ego-Morphing** — Whitman's technique of making the reader mentally project themselves into the ad as the person who already owns/uses the product (Ch 5, 17)

**Elaboration Likelihood Model (ELM)** — Dual-route persuasion model: the central route (logic, evidence) for high-involvement buyers and the peripheral route (cues, heuristics) for low-involvement buyers (Ch 1, 5)

**Extreme Specificity** — Using precise numbers and concrete details instead of vague claims to build credibility ("saves 33.7% on fuel" vs "saves money") (Ch 6, 13)

**Fascination Bullets** — Benefit-loaded bullet points that create intense curiosity; each one is a micro-headline (Ch 11)

**Fear Appeal** — Activating a specific fear, then immediately presenting your product as the resolution (Ch 1, 5, 16)

**Fog Index** — Readability formula measuring grade level of writing; mass-market copy targets 8th-10th grade (Ch 9)

**Heuristics** — Mental shortcuts consumers use to make quick decisions — price = quality, brand = safety, popularity = good (Ch 1)

**Hot Lists** — Whitman's compiled checklists: 22 Response Superchargers, 9 Value Conveyors, 13 Buying Ease Tactics, 11 Coupon Boosters, 46-Point Killer Ad Checklist (Ch 18)

**Inoculation Theory** — Preemptively addressing a weak form of competitors' arguments so consumers resist those arguments later (Ch 6)

**Keyed Advertising** — Hopkins' term for tracking ad performance through unique codes, coupons, or URLs to measure ROI scientifically (Ch 13)

**Life-Force 8 (LF8)** — Whitman's eight biologically hardwired human desires: survival, food/drink, freedom from fear/pain, sexual companionship, comfort, superiority, protection of loved ones, social approval (Ch 1)

**Means-End Chain** — Linking product attributes → functional benefits → emotional payoff in a logical chain the reader follows (Ch 1, 5)

**Mental Movies** — Whitman's technique of using specific, visual, sensory language to make the reader experience the product in their imagination before buying (Ch 7, 17)

**Message Sidedness** — Using both positive claims and minor admissions/concessions to appear more credible (Ch 6)

**Ogilvy Visual Flow** — The proven reading pattern: image → caption → headline → body copy (Ch 12)

**PVAs (Positive Visual Adjectives)** — Descriptive words chosen specifically to install vivid mental imagery — "steaming," "crispy," "velvety" (Ch 7)

**Psychological Pricing** — Using odd-number endings ($9.97, $19.99) for value perception; round numbers ($50, $1,000) for prestige perception (Ch 4)

**Reason-Why Copy** — Hopkins' approach of giving concrete, specific reasons why the product works and why the reader should buy now (Ch 6, 10)

**Response Superchargers** — Whitman's 22 proven tactics for boosting ad response: short sentences, long copy, visual adjectives, testimonials, deadlines, free gifts, etc. (Ch 18)

**Reverse Type** — White text on dark background; reduces readability by up to 50% — avoid for body copy (Ch 12)

**Seeds of Curiosity** — Sugarman's technique of planting short phrases at paragraph ends ("But there's more…", "Now here's the best part…") to maintain reading momentum (Ch 3)

**Slippery Slide** — Sugarman's core concept: every element of the ad must compel the reader to read the next line, creating unstoppable reading momentum (Ch 3)

**Social Proof** — Using testimonials, user counts, endorsements, and crowd behavior to validate the purchase decision (Ch 6)

**Tension-Desire-Action Loop** — Whitman's formula: unmet need → psychological tension → desire → action to satisfy the desire (Ch 1)

**Transtheoretical Model** — Six stages of persuasion: precontemplation → contemplation → preparation → action → maintenance → termination (Ch 1)

**Trial Close** — Inviting the prospect to test the product risk-free before committing, reducing purchase anxiety (Ch 19)

**Unique Selling Proposition (USP)** — Rosser Reeves' concept: the single most compelling reason to buy from you that competitors cannot claim (Ch 2, 8)

**Value Conveyors** — Whitman's 9 tactics for making price seem reasonable: sale framing, coupons, price diminishment, amortization, value boosting, scarcity deadlines, psychological pricing (Ch 4, 18)

**White-Wrap Isolation** — Surrounding your ad with white space to increase attention value by up to 76% versus a fully packed layout (Ch 12)
